# Chips-Customer-Purchase-Analysis
Analysis of customer purchasing behaviour in the chips category, using Excel and Power Query to identify key customer segments, top brands, preferred pack sizes, and growth opportunities.
# Customer Purchasing Performance Behaviour

## Project Overview

This project analyzes transaction and loyalty data to understand customer purchasing behaviour in the chips category. The analysis transforms raw data into actionable commercial insights for category review, customer targeting, product ranging, and promotional planning.

## Business Problem

The client lacks visibility into how different customer segments engage with the chips category. This makes it difficult to determine:

- Which customer segments to target
- Which brands to promote
- Which pack sizes to prioritize
- Where the strongest growth opportunities exist

## Project Objectives

- Identify the highest-value customer segments.
- Understand the brands and pack sizes driving sales.
- Compare purchasing behaviour across customer lifestyles.
- Identify growth opportunities within the chips category.
- Develop practical commercial recommendations for category review.

## Key Performance Highlights

- **Total chip sales:** $135,104
- **Total customers:** 20,680
- **Average spend per customer:** 6.42
- **Top customer segment:** Mainstream
- **Top-performing brand:** Thins
- **Top-performing pack size:** 170g
- **Highest-value lifestyle groups:** Retirees and Older Families

## Key Dashboard Insights

### Customer Segment Performance

Mainstream customers are the strongest-performing segment, generating **$54,603** in sales. Budget customers contribute **$45,747**, while Premium customers contribute **$34,754**.

This indicates that Mainstream customers should be the primary focus for customer targeting, promotional planning, and in-store visibility.

### Brand Performance

The leading chip brands by sales are:

1. **Thins:** $24,466
2. **Doritos:** $21,815
3. **Cobs:** $19,224
4. **Smiths:** $16,840
5. **WW:** $4,995

Thins, Doritos, and Cobs should form the core of the category assortment and promotional strategy.

### Pack Size Performance

The strongest-performing pack sizes are:

- **170g:** $29,239
- **175g:** $26,470
- **110g:** $19,224
- **330g:** $9,416
- **160g:** $2,576

The 170g and 175g packs are the strongest performers, suggesting that shoppers prefer familiar mid-sized pack formats.

### Lifestyle Performance

The highest-performing lifestyle groups are:

- **Retirees:** $18,262
- **Older Families:** $18,173
- **Young Families:** $11,641
- **Midage:** $8,882
- **New Families:** $2,936

Retirees and Older Families represent attractive target groups for value, convenience, and repeat-purchase messaging.

## Commercial Recommendations

- Prioritize Mainstream customers in promotional planning and category activation.
- Use Thins, Doritos, and Cobs as key promotional and ranging brands.
- Build promotions around the 170g and 175g pack sizes.
- Target Retirees and Older Families with convenience, value, and routine-purchase messaging.
- Use Budget-focused offers to protect volume without over-discounting high-performing Mainstream products.
- Review low-performing brands and pack sizes for reduced shelf space, delisting, or targeted test promotions.
- Track segment sales, average spend, brand mix, and pack-size mix regularly to measure category performance.

## Conclusion

The analysis indicates that category growth should be led by the Mainstream segment, supported by high-performing brands such as Thins, Doritos, and Cobs, alongside the strongest mid-sized pack formats.
A focused, segment-led assortment and promotional strategy can improve shopper relevance, shelf productivity, and commercial performance.
